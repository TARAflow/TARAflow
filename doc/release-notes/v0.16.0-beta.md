## TARAflow 0.16.0-beta

ISO/SAE 21434 concept phase in TARAflow: Item Definition, cybersecurity goals and claims, and the TARA verification. Plus your own mitigation catalog, a closed loop that reports only what matters, and a new issue-tracker integration.

> New project schema **11** — a project is converted when it is opened (a backup of the original is written next to it) and then no longer opens in 0.15.x.

### ISO/SAE 21434 work products

- **Item Definition** (Overview → *Definition*): purpose and functions, item boundary, preliminary architecture, operational environment, constraints — each with text, a source document and links into the DFD and the assets. Functions with their lifecycle phases. **Assumptions** (ASM-nn) with owner, review date and status; trust boundaries link the assumptions they rely on. Missing mandatory parts are reported, and the report has a new *Item Definition* chapter.
- **Cybersecurity goals and claims:** a new tab *Goal / Claim* in the risk dialog — a goal (CSG-nn, optional CAL) for every risk you reduce, proposed from the asset's security goal; a claim (CLM-nn) for every risk you retain (based on assumptions) or share (with the responsible party). The *Goals & Claims* dialog in the Risk tab shows everything per threat scenario, proposes missing goals and lists the findings. New report chapter and a goal / claim column in the traceability matrix.
- **TARA verification:** record the verification of the TARA in the Audit tab — the four points of RQ-09-07 as a checklist, result and findings, sealed with a hash of the analysis. TARAflow tells you when the analysis changed since, or when the verification is older than 90 days; the status shows in the Overview, the phase bar and the report. Ticket and status changes of mitigations do not count as a change of the analysis.
- **Risk dialog:** Feasibility | Impact | Risk, each with where it comes from (attack-tree path and attack potential, damage scenario); the residual is rated with the five attack-potential factors, listed least likely first with the familiar colour dot.
- **Hazards:** a person can be rated *none (no injury, S0)*; severities show their ISO 26262 class (S0–S3).
- **Attack trees:** the ids AT-n / AT-n.m used by threats, risks and the report now appear in the attack-tree tab.

Goals, claims and the verification appear in ISO/SAE 21434 projects only.

### For every project

- **Project mitigations (M-C-nnn):** write your own mitigations once — text, STRIDE category, optional attack vectors and the DFD properties they set — and use them on any threat or risk like a catalog entry. They are kept on regeneration, can be merged and are checked by the closed loop. New free-text mitigations are no longer offered; existing ones can be converted.
- **Closed loop:** the DFD notification panel now separates *planned* mitigations (information) from mitigations marked done that the DFD contradicts (warning / conflict) — in the Smoke Detector example 1 notification instead of 81. *Apply* is offered only for a mitigation that is done, and can restore a value that was changed back. Attack-path risks of ISO projects reach the DFD through the attack tree's asset.
- **Jira:** one Integration tab. The API token stays in the OS keychain, bound to the Jira address — a project file can never point it elsewhere. Ticket states sync when the Risk tab opens or on *Sync ticket status*; the mitigation status follows its ticket in both directions, and a ticket closed as *won't fix* marks the mitigation rejected with the ticket as reason.
- **Your name stays on your machine:** the commit author and your Jira e-mail are no longer written into the project file — every team member uses their own.
- **Signed stays signed:** once an audit repository has a signer manifest, every commit in it is signed, for every project. The commit dialog tells you before the click when a key or an authorization is missing.
- **Overview and phase bar:** every phase card explains its status and opens its tab; badges on the phase tabs; the Risk tab's findings (e.g. a risk to reduce without a mitigation) are now visible.
- **Report:** the PDF has all chapters the other formats have; risk levels in reports of classic projects follow the risk scale (the summary no longer calls almost every risk critical).

### What you should do

1. Open your projects in 0.16 — they are converted to schema 11 (backup alongside). Keep 0.15.2 only for projects you still need to open there.
2. **Jira:** open the Integration tab and run the connection test once with your own account; the token is stored for this Jira address.
3. **Audit repositories with a signer manifest:** make sure your signing key is set in the audit configuration (Config → Signing) — commits there are always signed.
4. **Azure DevOps** has no connection in 0.16; it comes back in 0.17. A project that depends on it should stay on 0.15.2 until then.
